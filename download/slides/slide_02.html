Let's set the stage. Every public web form is a target. SQL injection, cross-site scripting, and path traversal are automated, scripted, and routinely fired at every endpoint on the internet — and they top the OWASP rankings year after year, with SQLi sitting at number one. Brute-force login attacks are even cheaper: parallel credential stuffing runs cost pennies and hammer auth endpoints around the clock. The numbers tell the rest of the story: eighty-three percent of web apps ship with at least one vulnerability, and the average breach goes undetected for one hundred and seventy-seven days. Traditional WAFs block-and-forget — they drop the request and move on, never learning who is behind it or what they're after. That intelligence gap is exactly what DeceptiWAF was built to close.
